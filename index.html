01 The Shape of the Business

One location. Two kinds of buyer.

A retail customer finds you online and buys. A wholesale account orders on terms. And both of them walk into the same store — a trade buyer picking up a restock in the same aisle as a consumer buying one unit. Most platforms make you run that as three separate systems.

The cost of three systems
• Catalog maintained in two or three places
• Inventory drift between web and shop floor
• The same buyer existing as two customer records
• Staff toggling between apps mid-transaction
• Month-end reconciliation across separate payment stacks
• Every new channel is a new integration project
What one platform changes
• One product catalog, one inventory pool per location
• One customer record, whether they buy retail or wholesale
• One payment stack across web and counter
• One reporting layer across all three motions
• Flow, Markets, and Shopify Payments work for B2B and DTC
• Adding a channel is configuration, not a rebuild
The thesis You aren't buying three products. You're buying one system with three front doors — which is why the back office stays the size it is while the channels multiply.

03 B2B

Wholesale is native, not an app you bolt on.

Company accounts, custom pricing, and payment terms are built into the same store as your retail business — same products, same inventory, same admin.

Company accounts
A company holds its locations (up to 50) and the buyers at each, with Location Admin / Ordering Only / View Only roles. Pricing, terms, and permissions attach to that record.
Catalogs + price lists
A catalog gates what they can see — products outside it can't be added to cart. A price list sets what they pay, as a % off base or fixed per variant, with currency-specific pricing.
Ordering rules
Quantity minimums, maximums, and increments enforced in the cart. A must-order-in-12s product blocks a 30-unit checkout. No coupon codes, no manual policing.
Terms & invoicing
Net terms, due-on-fulfillment, or pay-upfront per company. Buyers on terms check out without entering payment; Shopify invoices, sends reminders, and flags past-due. PO numbers carry to order and invoice.
One storefront, two audiences
A logged-in wholesale buyer sees their catalog, their pricing, their payment options — and you can conditionally show different content. Retail shoppers see none of it. Shared inventory, one source of truth, one team.
What Plus adds specifically
Foundational B2B is on every paid plan as of April 2026. Plus removes the ceilings: unlimited catalogs, direct catalog assignment per company and location, and deposits and partial payments — plus contextual checkout customization.

05 The In-Store Wholesale Buyer

When a trade buyer walks in.

This is the specific mechanic worth understanding up front, because it's the one place the three channels don't fuse automatically. B2B catalog pricing does not apply on its own inside a POS checkout — price lists attach to a logged-in storefront session, and POS doesn't participate in that session. Here are the three ways merchants solve it.

Path 1 · Native, no apps

Draft order from POS

Build the cart at the register, attach the B2B customer and company location, save as a draft. The draft reflects that company's pricing, payment terms, and checkout options automatically — then it's created as an order or paid by invoice on terms.

Best for occasional walk-ins
Path 2 · POS UI extension

Catalog pricing at the register

Purpose-built apps read your existing native B2B catalogs and apply that company's pricing in the POS cart. Staff pick the customer and company location; correct pricing applies as tracked line-item adjustments. Some also enforce quantity rules and capture PO numbers.

Best if trade walk-ins are routine
Path 3 · Concierge

Place it for them, like a phone order

Staff ask "do you have a company account with us?" — then place the order for the buyer from Shopify admin, exactly as they would take it over the phone. The company's catalog, pricing, and quantity rules all apply, and you charge a card already vaulted on the account or send it to their terms.

Best for high-touch accounts
Our recommendation Start by mapping how often a trade buyer actually transacts at the counter versus ordering online. If it's a daily motion, go straight to a POS extension so pricing is never a judgment call. If it's occasional, native draft orders cost nothing and keep the account, terms, and PO trail intact. Either way, the company record, catalog, and terms you set up for B2B online are the same ones the store uses — you're not building wholesale twice.
